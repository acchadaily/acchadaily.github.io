Paytm gets RBI's in-principle nod to run wallets again; shares close at ₹1,939.70, about 10% below the ₹2,150 IPO price

Two and a half years after the Payments Bank crackdown took its wallet away, Paytm is a step closer to getting it back, and its stock is nearly back where it listed.

What RBI approved

The Reserve Bank of India has given Paytm Payments Services Ltd (PPSL), a wholly owned subsidiary of Paytm's parent One 97 Communications, an in-principle approval to issue and run prepaid payment instruments. That category covers digital wallets, the product that made Paytm a household name.

It is not the final green light yet. PPSL has to submit a system and cybersecurity audit within six months, or the approval lapses, and it still needs final authorisation from the RBI before it can issue a single wallet. Paytm applied in July 2026.

Why it's a big deal

Paytm's wallet effectively went dark after the RBI's January 2024 action against Paytm Payments Bank, which barred it from taking fresh deposits, including wallet top-ups. According to Entrackr, the RBI later cancelled the Payments Bank's licence on 24 April 2026. A wallet licence under a different company is the clearest sign yet of a regulatory comeback.

The stock story

Paytm shares closed at ₹1,939.70 on Friday, up about 7.7% over two sessions and close to a 52-week high of ₹1,960. That is about 9.8% below the ₹2,150 price at which it listed in 2021, and more than double its 52-week low of ₹930.60 in March.

The business has also turned around: Q2 FY27 revenue rose 28.5% to ₹2,648 crore and net profit came in at ₹225 crore, its sixth profitable quarter in a row. Brokerage Bernstein raised its target price to ₹2,200 from ₹1,500.

What's next

Watch for the audit and the RBI's final authorisation. For users, it could mean the familiar Paytm wallet returning for small, quick payments alongside UPI.
