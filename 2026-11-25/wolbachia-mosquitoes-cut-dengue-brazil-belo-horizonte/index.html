Releasing Wolbachia mosquitoes cut dengue by 68.5% in a Brazilian city during the country's worst outbreak on record

A randomised trial in Belo Horizonte found that mosquitoes carrying a harmless bacterium also reduced chikungunya by 73%.

  • Funded mostly by the US NIH and Brazil's health ministry

Mosquitoes that block viruses

Wolbachia is a natural bacterium that, once inside Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, makes it hard for them to spread viruses, and the mosquitoes pass it to their young. Researchers split Belo Horizonte into 58 school-based zones: half got Wolbachia mosquito releases plus normal pest control, half only the usual spraying.

Releases ran from January 2021 to May 2022, and 5,757 children aged 6 to 11 were followed.

Strong results

Dengue infections fell by 68.5% and chikungunya by 73.3% in the release zones, even though the trial overlapped with Brazil's biggest dengue outbreak ever recorded. The findings, from a team including Fiocruz, UFMG, the World Mosquito Program and Johns Hopkins, were presented at a US tropical medicine meeting on 21 November.
