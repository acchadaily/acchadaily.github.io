A Hyderabad paediatrician helps cut a rare-disease drug's cost from up to ₹9 crore a year to about ₹2.5 lakh; seven children get a free year of treatment

Dr Sivaranjani Santosh refused to give up after drug makers ignored her, and with patient advocates found an affordable source of tiratricol.

accha? daily · 25 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
7children to get a free year of medicine
  • Drug: tiratricol, for Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome
  • Cost down from up to ₹9 crore to ~₹2.5 lakh a year per child
  • First free doses to be handed over on 30 November

A medicine out of reach

Tiratricol treats Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome, a rare genetic condition in which thyroid hormone cannot reach the brain, affecting movement and development. The drug was reported to cost as much as ₹9 crore a year, and the mother of Dr Sivaranjani's young patient Arav had to find ₹12–13 lakh for a single month imported from Germany.

Advocates step in

After her appeals to companies went unanswered, the doctor posted Arav's case online in September. Access to Medicines India and the Netherlands-based Pharmaceutical Accountability Foundation then helped locate a far cheaper option, about ₹2.5 lakh per child a year.

Funds are being raised in India and the Netherlands so that seven children, including five at CMC Vellore, get a year's supply free. The first handover is set for 30 November.
