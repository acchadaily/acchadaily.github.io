FSSAI declares a Bisleri water batch from Goa unsafe and clears prosecution; Bisleri says the ban covers just one batch

A retest found traces of chemicals called PAHs in one batch of Bisleri bottled water. Bisleri says the batch was made in September 2025 and is no longer on shelves.

accha? daily · 25 November 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp
In short
1 batchGO 270, made 27 Sep 2025
  • First test showed bromate at 0.012 mg/L vs a 0.01 mg/L limit
  • Retest found PAHs; batch GO 270 banned from sale
  • Goa FDA has tested 80+ packaged water samples since April 2025

What the regulator found

Goa's food regulator first flagged a Bisleri water sample for bromate, a chemical that can form when water is disinfected. The first lab test showed 0.012 mg per litre, just above the 0.01 mg limit.

Bisleri appealed, and the sample went to the National Food Laboratory in Navi Mumbai. That lab found bromate was within limits, but it detected small amounts of polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs), a group of chemicals linked to burning and fuel.

FSSAI's action

On 23 November, FSSAI said that because of the PAH result, the batch was unsafe. It banned the sale of batch GO 270, asked Bisleri to find the root cause, and approved prosecution of Bisleri International under the food safety law.

Reports say FSSAI has also taken samples from other big packaged water brands and may act against any that fail.

Bisleri's side

In a fresh statement on 24 November, Bisleri said the ban applies only to that one batch, made at its Goa plant on 27 September 2025. It said other samples from the same source had passed tests, and that it had pointed out problems with the results and asked for a PAH retest back in February 2026.

The company said it will keep working with FSSAI but will defend itself in the legal process.

Should you worry?

Goa's FDA says the batch in question was tested in October 2025 and is no longer being sold. It has tested more than 80 packaged water samples since April 2025 as the row widened into a check on bottled water across the state.
