Women of an Uttarakhand village bring 28 acres of bare forest back to life, with no forest fire there in over 20 years

Led by 75-year-old Bhagirathi Devi, known as 'Van Amma', the women of Manar in Champawat turned an overgrazed hillside into a healthy oak forest that the forest department now holds up as a fire-prevention model.

In short
11.6 haforest restored since 2000
  • About 28 acres (11.6 hectares) revived in Champawat district
  • No fire reported in the restored area for more than 20 years
  • Champawat Forest Department calls it a fire-prevention model

From barren slope to living forest

In 2000 the woods around Manar village had been stripped by grazing and tree cutting, and women walked 7–8 km each day for fodder and firewood. Bhagirathi Devi gathered them into a van panchayat, a community forest council, and became its first sarpanch.

They fenced the land with stones and thorny bushes, pulled out invasive lantana and planted native trees such as banj oak, with help from the BAIF Development Research Foundation. Grazing was stopped for two years while the saplings took hold.

Guardians against fire

Every year before the dry season, villagers clear fallen leaves and pine needles, and Bhagirathi Devi still patrols daily. Residents say the restored patch has not burned once since the effort began, even as fires sweep other parts of the state. A 2022 study found the forest under their care was in better health than the woods outside it.
