S&P trims India's FY28 growth forecast to 7% and expects the RBI to raise rates again in December

With retail inflation at 6.1% in October, above the RBI's comfort band, S&P sees the repo rate climbing to 6% by March 2027 — which would mean costlier loans.

WhatsApp
In short
6.1%retail inflation in October
  • FY28 growth forecast cut from 7.2% to 7%
  • Repo rate seen at 6% by end-FY27
  • Rupee seen at 94.5/$ by March 2027

What S&P said

S&P Global Ratings kept its growth forecast for this year (FY27) at 7% but cut next year's (FY28) by 0.2 points to 7%. India grew 7.8% in FY26 and, with Vietnam, is still expected to be the fastest-growing economy in Asia-Pacific.

S&P expects inflation to average 5.3% this year, up from just 2% last year, mainly because of costlier inputs, a weak monsoon linked to El Niño and high energy prices from the West Asia conflict.

What it means for loans

The RBI raised its repo rate to 5.50% in October. S&P expects another 0.25-point hike at the December meeting and one more early in 2027, taking the rate to 6%. Higher rates usually mean higher EMIs on floating-rate home and car loans, but better returns on fixed deposits. S&P also sees the rupee weakening to about 94.5 per dollar by March 2027.
