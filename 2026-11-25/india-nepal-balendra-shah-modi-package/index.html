India announces a ₹15,000 crore package for Nepal and opens Kandla and Paradip ports to it as Modi and Balendra Shah sign 8 agreements

Nepal PM Balendra Shah's first state visit ended on 23 November with a new Ayodhya–Janakpur train, a border bridge and deals on space, health and training.

Money and ports

India announced a development package of 24,000 crore Nepali rupees — about ₹15,000 crore — after talks between PM Narendra Modi and Nepal's PM Balendra Shah in New Delhi. Much of it is meant for families hit by floods and for making Nepal better prepared for disasters.

India will also let landlocked Nepal use the Kandla and Paradip ports for its transit trade, giving it more routes to the sea.

Train, bridge and eight agreements

The two leaders virtually flagged off the Saryu-Kamala Express, a new weekly train between Ayodhya Cantt and Janakpur Dham. They opened a road bridge at Dharchula on the Uttarakhand border and laid foundation stones for an integrated check post at Banbasa and the Lower Arun hydropower project.

At least eight agreements were signed, covering satellite communication with ISRO, health, Ayurveda, urban development, civil-service training, police training and livestock. India also plans an IIT offshore campus in Nepal and more scholarships. Shah called India a 'dependable' neighbour that is often the first to help in disasters.
