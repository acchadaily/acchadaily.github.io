Gukesh starts his World Chess Championship defence with a 38-move draw against Sindarov in Geneva

The 14-game, $2.5 million match is level at 0.5–0.5. Game 2 is today at 7:30 pm IST, with Gukesh playing White.

Game 1

India's D Gukesh, the defending world champion, had the black pieces on Tuesday, 24 November, against Uzbekistan's Javokhir Sindarov. Both players are 20. Gukesh chose the solid Berlin Defence, and Sindarov steered into the Berlin endgame, a line he had never played before.

Sindarov tried rare moves to unsettle him, and Gukesh spent a long time thinking, but it was the challenger who ended up short of time. The game ended in a draw by repetition after 38 moves. Sindarov claimed the draw the wrong way, making his move first, and the arbiter took two minutes off his clock, though that did not change the result.

The match

The match is in Geneva, Switzerland, over 14 games; the first to 7.5 points wins. Sindarov is rated 2782 and Gukesh 2710, so the champion goes in as the lower-rated player. If it is 7–7, rapid games decide it. The prize fund is $2.5 million, with $200,000 for every game won.

💡 Explained

How does the World Chess Championship match work?

It is a one-on-one match between the reigning champion and a challenger who earns the spot by winning the Candidates Tournament. This year's match in Geneva has 14 classical games, with two hours each for the first 40 moves. A win is worth one point and a draw half a point, so the first player to reach 7.5 points takes the title. If it ends 7–7, four faster rapid games decide the champion.
