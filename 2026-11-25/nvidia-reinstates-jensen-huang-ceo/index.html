Nvidia brings back Jensen Huang as CEO 11 days after he quit; shares jump 31% in a day, then slip about 6%

Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate its founder after President Trump publicly pushed for his return. Its investigation into the reasons he resigned is still going on.

In short

  • Huang resigned on 12 November and was reinstated on 23 November
  • Shares rose about 31% on Monday, the biggest gain since 2003
  • Stock fell about 5.9% on Tuesday as the rally faded

What happened

On Monday, 23 November, Nvidia's board said Jensen Huang was back as president, CEO and board member with immediate effect. The vote was unanimous. The board said it had held a series of meetings with him and was satisfied with his 'candor and ability to lead'.

Huang co-founded the world's most important AI chip company and had run it for more than three decades. He stepped down on 12 November, and chief financial officer Colette Kress took over as interim CEO. She now goes back to her CFO job, and Harvey Jones stays on as board chair.

Why he left in the first place

Huang resigned after reports about extramarital relationships, including one with a woman whom US intelligence reportedly believed was a Chinese spy, NBC News reported. In a memo to staff earlier in November he admitted he had been unfaithful to his wife.

Nvidia's own statement says the board consulted senior US government officials and concluded that bringing him back poses no national security risk. A committee of independent directors is still investigating, and the board says that probe will run to the end.

Trump's role and the market rollercoaster

Trump said he spoke to the board 'directly and indirectly' and that Huang 'did nothing wrong'. Critics were unhappy: Senator Elizabeth Warren called it a 'reckless decision', and governance experts told Reuters the board hurt its claim to independence by acting before its probe was finished.

Investors cheered at first. Nvidia shares rose about 31% on Monday, their biggest one-day gain since 2003, according to Bloomberg. The rally cooled on Tuesday, with the stock falling about 5.9% as attention turned to political risks and scrutiny of Nvidia's chip sales to China.

What changes now

Nvidia is setting up a new board committee on national security and export controls, made up only of independent directors, and is hiring a new chief compliance officer. Huang said he takes the job back 'with the greatest humility and renewed commitment'. For the AI industry, which runs largely on Nvidia chips, the bigger question is whether this ends the uncertainty or just pauses it until the investigation reports.
