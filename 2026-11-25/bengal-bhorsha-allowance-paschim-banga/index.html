Bengal will pay jobless graduates ₹2,000 a month from January under the new Bhorsha scheme; cabinet also clears renaming the state 'Paschim Banga'

Chief Minister Suvendu Adhikari's cabinet replaced the old Yuvasathi scheme and will check about 54 lakh existing beneficiaries by 31 December. The name-change resolution goes to the Assembly on 9 December.

In short
₹2,000a month for unemployed graduates aged 21–45
  • ₹2,000/month for jobless graduates, ₹1,500 for non-graduates
  • About 54 lakh old Yuvasathi beneficiaries to be verified by 31 Dec
  • Paschim Banga resolution in the Assembly on 9 Dec

Who gets how much

West Bengal's new 'Bhorsha Karmasuchi' will put money straight into the bank accounts of unemployed young people every month, starting in January. Graduates, and diploma holders from government vocational institutes and ITIs, will get ₹2,000 a month. Unemployed young people who are not graduates will get ₹1,500.

To qualify, you must be between 21 and 45 years old. A family can have at most two people on the scheme, and those already getting ₹3,000 under the state's Annapurna Yojana are left out. Payments will land in the second week of each month, and the scheme will be formally launched at an event in February.

Replacing Yuvasathi

The scheme takes the place of the previous Trinamool Congress government's Yuvasathi scheme. The state will verify the roughly 54 lakh people already registered under the old scheme by 31 December. People who have not applied yet can do so through the 'Apnar Sarkar Apnar Pashe' programme, with forms going to the Youth Welfare Department.

The BJP's manifesto had promised more. The government says the amounts will rise to ₹3,000 and ₹2,000 from the 2028-29 financial year. Adhikari asked young people to 'be patient for some time', adding: 'We will keep our commitment.'

'Paschim Banga' in English too

At the same 23 November announcement, Adhikari said the cabinet had approved renaming the state 'Paschim Banga' — the same name in English as in Bengali. A resolution will be placed in the Assembly on 9 December, during a winter session that runs from 4 to 14 December.

If the Assembly passes it, the proposal must still be approved by the Centre and Parliament before the new name becomes official. Earlier attempts by the Left Front in 1999 and the Trinamool government in 2011 and 2016 did not get the Centre's approval.
