Surat schoolboy's AI tool to forecast groundwater wins a national contest of over 6,100 projects

Dheyay Shah's JALAAKAR uses borewell records, rainfall and soil data to estimate how much water lies under each taluka.

accha? daily · 25 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
6,100+projects in the contest
  • Tool name: JALAAKAR
  • Uses borewell, rainfall and soil data
  • Won vivo Ignite 2026 from 6,100+ entries

Mapping water we can't see

Much of India relies on groundwater that is being drawn faster than rain refills it. Dheyay Shah, a student from Surat, built a machine-learning tool that studies past borewell levels, rainfall patterns and soil types to project groundwater conditions taluka by taluka.

The idea is to help farmers, communities and water managers decide where to pump less and where to save.

Top of the class

JALAAKAR won the national title at vivo Ignite 2026, a science challenge for school students that drew more than 6,100 entries judged on creativity, feasibility, cost and social impact. Experts note the tool still needs field testing before wider use, and that forecasts cannot replace real measurements.
