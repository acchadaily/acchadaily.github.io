COP31 in Antalya ends with no climate deal after record overtime, as countries split over moving away from fossil fuels

The longest UN climate summit ever broke up on 22 November without its main text — the first such collapse since Copenhagen in 2009.

How it fell apart

The COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, ended on Sunday, 22 November, without agreement on their main political text. The meeting ran deep into overtime, becoming the longest COP in history, but because decisions need every country's consent, the draft could not be adopted.

The fight was over fossil fuels. An early draft called for faster action to phase them out, but that wording was dropped. The European Union refused to accept a text with no mention of fossil fuels, and a group led by France and Panama — with members from Europe, Africa, Latin America and the Pacific — rejected the later draft as too weak. Oil producers such as Saudi Arabia pushed back hard.

What was on the table

Later drafts had tried softer goals: capturing billions of tonnes of carbon dioxide from the air every year by 2035, getting more than a third of the world's energy from electricity by 2035, and targets for renewables and energy efficiency. None of it was adopted as a package.

The United States sent no team for the second year in a row after quitting the Paris Agreement.

What leaders said

UN chief António Guterres said there might be no consensus in the room, but 'there is no ambiguity in the science.' Summit president Murat Kurum said, 'We did everything we could.' Panama's environment minister Juan Carlos Navarro said the world could not keep being 'held hostage by petroleum producers.'

Even if every country's current climate plan is carried out, the world is heading for just over 2.5°C of warming — well above the Paris goal of 1.5°C.
