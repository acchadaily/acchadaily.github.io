47,473 acres of Alaskan islands where Pacific walrus rest are now protected for good

After a decade of work, a conservation easement near Point Lay shields North America's largest walrus haul-out from disturbance and development.

  • About 47,473 acres near Point Lay protected
  • 200,000+ walrus hauled out there in 2007
  • Local subsistence and cultural use continues

A beach for walrus

Pacific walrus once rested on Arctic sea ice, but as the ice became unreliable they began crowding onto barrier islands off Point Lay in northwest Alaska. In 2007 more than 200,000 females and young hauled out there, and they have come back every year since.

Crowded beaches are fragile: disturbances from people or planes can trigger stampedes that kill calves.

Protected forever

The Conservation Fund bought a conservation easement from an Alaska Native corporation, and the Interior Alaska Land Trust will hold it and step up monitoring. The deal covers the whole island haul-out, nearby mainland and part of Kasegaluk Lagoon, which also shelters polar bears, spotted seals and belugas, while keeping traditional subsistence use open to local people.
