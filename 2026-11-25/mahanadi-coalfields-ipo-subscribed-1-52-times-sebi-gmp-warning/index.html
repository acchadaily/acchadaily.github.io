IPO watch: Mahanadi Coalfields' ₹8,981 crore IPO scrapes through at 1.52 times; SEBI tells market players to stop sharing 'GMP'

Big institutions saved Coal India's subsidiary's IPO as retail buyers stayed away, while SEBI warned that grey market premiums are no guide to listing gains.

Mahanadi just about makes it

The ₹8,981 crore IPO of Mahanadi Coalfields, a Coal India subsidiary, closed on Monday with bids for 1.52 times the shares on offer. Institutional investors bid 4.95 times their quota, but the retail portion was only 46% filled and wealthy individuals 66%.

The whole issue was a sale by Coal India, so Mahanadi gets no money. Shares are priced at ₹129–136, allotment is due today (25 November) and listing is set for 27 November.

SEBI on GMP

SEBI has asked registered market players to stop publishing or circulating grey market premium (GMP) figures for upcoming IPOs. It says these unofficial numbers are speculative, happen outside regulated markets, and should not be used to guess listing prices.

Coming up

Foodlink F&B Holdings opens on 26 November with a ₹192 crore fresh issue, and Integris Medtech opens on 27 November with a ₹600 crore fresh issue plus an offer for sale.

💡 Explained

What is a grey market premium (GMP)?

Before a company lists on the stock exchange, some people informally trade its IPO shares in an unofficial 'grey market'. The extra price they pay over the IPO price is called the grey market premium. Many investors watch it to guess how much a stock might gain on listing day. But these deals have no rules, no guarantee and no protection if something goes wrong, which is why SEBI now says GMP should not be treated as a guide to returns.
