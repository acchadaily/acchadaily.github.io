India's record 720 isn't enough: Mitchell and Phillips bat out the last day as New Zealand draw the Wellington Test

India needed eight wickets on the last day but took only three on a flat Basin Reserve pitch. The series is level 0-0 going into Christchurch on Friday.

How the last day went

New Zealand began Monday, 23 November, at 119 for 2, still 233 runs behind India's 720 for 9 declared. They made no attempt to chase and simply tried to survive. India removed Devon Conway and Rachin Ravindra in the first hour, and Anshul Kamboj had Tom Blundell out soon after lunch, leaving the hosts 176 for 5.

That was India's last breakthrough. Daryl Mitchell (49 not out off 186 balls) and Glenn Phillips (26 not out off 126 balls) blocked out the rest, and the players shook hands with about an hour left. New Zealand finished on 239 for 5 from 120 overs.

A match of big numbers

India's 720 was the highest total ever made by any team in a Test in New Zealand. KL Rahul scored 136, and Rishabh Pant hit 128 off 153 balls. Jasprit Bumrah, back after a year out, got Kiwi captain Tom Latham in the first over of both innings.

New Zealand's first-innings fightback was just as important. From 70 for 5, Henry Nicholls made 175 not out and added 216 with Phillips to reach 368. Nicholls was named Player of the Match.

The pitch and the points

The surface flattened out over five days. Captain Shubman Gill admitted it was always 'going to be a challenge to get 10 wickets on that pitch'. Nicholls called the draw 'a pleasing result'.

Both teams get four World Test Championship points. New Zealand stay third behind South Africa and Australia, and India are fourth, so they will want a win in the second and final Test at Hagley Oval, Christchurch, from 27 November.
