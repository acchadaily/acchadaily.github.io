IIT Roorkee scientists create a brand-new spring that doubles summer drinking water for a Tehri village, to 1,000 litres a day

By mapping the rocks under a Himalayan hillside, researchers found where groundwater was trapped and opened a path for it to reach Unchaun village.

accha? daily · 25 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
2xlean-season drinking water
  • Village: Unchaun, Kirtinagar block, Tehri Garhwal
  • Supply rose from ~500 to 1,000+ litres a day in the dry season
  • About 82% of the area's recharge comes from monsoon rain

Reading the mountain

Hill villages in Uttarakhand depend on rain-fed springs that shrink as summer arrives. A team from IIT Roorkee's Earth Sciences department, led by Prof. Abhayanand Singh Maurya and backed by the Namami Gange mission, studied the rock layers of the Takoli Gad watershed to find those that store and carry water.

They also tracked how rain recharges the ground, finding most of it comes from the monsoon.

A spring where there was none

Trenches dug along the upper slope now slow down runoff so more rain soaks in. At a suitable water-bearing layer, the team made a controlled opening that lets groundwater surface as a new spring. Unchaun's lean-season supply has risen from about 500 litres a day to 1,000 litres or more. Researchers will keep checking its flow and quality across seasons.
