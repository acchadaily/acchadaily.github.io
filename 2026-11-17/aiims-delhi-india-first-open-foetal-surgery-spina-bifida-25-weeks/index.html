AIIMS Delhi performs India's first open foetal surgery to repair a baby's spine inside the womb, free of cost

A team of more than 30 doctors operated for about six hours at 25 weeks of pregnancy to fix the most severe form of spina bifida.

What the doctors did

On 12 November, surgeons at AIIMS New Delhi opened the uterus of a mother 25 weeks into her pregnancy, repaired the baby's exposed spinal cord and closed everything up so the pregnancy could continue, The Better India reports. The baby had lumbosacral myelomeningocele, the most serious form of spina bifida.

The roughly six-hour operation involved obstetricians, neurosurgeons, anaesthetists, plastic surgeons, radiologists and newborn specialists after more than five weeks of planning and simulations.

Why it's a big deal

Repairing the spine before birth can reduce nerve damage compared with surgery after delivery. The family paid nothing: it was covered under the government's Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram. Mother and baby are being closely monitored until birth.
