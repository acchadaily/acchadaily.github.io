Space

NASA's Voyager 1 will be one light-day from Earth tomorrow, 18 November: about 25.9 billion km away, 49 years after launch

A radio signal will take a full day to reach it, so a message and reply will take about 48 hours.

The milestone

Launched on 5 September 1977, Voyager 1 is the most distant human-made object. NASA says it will cross the one-light-day mark on 18 November, EarthSky reports. That's roughly 25.9 billion km, or about 173 times the distance between Earth and the Sun.

Nothing physical changes at that point, but it means light, and any radio command, needs 24 hours to get there.

Still going

Most of Voyager's instruments have been switched off to save power, but it keeps sending data from interstellar space. Its twin, Voyager 2, won't reach the same distance until around November 2035.
