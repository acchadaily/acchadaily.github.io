Your Wallet

Samsung and Xiaomi raise prices of 48 phone variants in India by ₹1,000–₹4,000; Galaxy A57 and Redmi Note 17 Pro see the biggest jumps

The increases took effect on 15–16 November and are being linked to a global surge in memory-chip prices driven by AI data centres.

What changed

Buying a new Samsung or Redmi phone just got costlier. Across about 48 variants, prices went up by ₹1,000 to ₹4,000 on 15 and 16 November, according to Inc42. Roughly 29–30 of those are Samsung models and 19 are Xiaomi's Redmi phones.

On Samsung's side, 14 Galaxy A-series variants (A07 5G, A17 5G, A36, A37, A56, A57 and F17 5G) went up, mostly by ₹2,000. Seven M17 5G, M17e and F70e 5G variants also rose by ₹2,000 each.

The steepest hikes

The Galaxy A57 saw the biggest jump at ₹4,000: the 8GB+256GB version now costs ₹66,999 and the 12GB+256GB version ₹76,499. For Xiaomi, the Redmi Note 17 Pro 5G took the largest hike, with the 8GB+128GB model now at ₹40,999 and the 8GB+256GB model at ₹43,999.

Some Redmi stock is reportedly still selling at old prices, so shoppers may find better deals for a short while.

Why prices are rising

The hikes are being tied to costlier memory chips. AI data centres are buying huge amounts of DRAM and NAND flash, pushing up prices for everyone else. Neither company has issued a statement giving the reason.

The demand problem

Higher prices are already hurting sales. Retailers' body AIMRA says smartphone volumes fell as much as 20% year-on-year during the 6–9 November festive window. If memory costs stay high, more brands could follow.
