'Forever chemicals' in England's rivers drop about 30% over a decade, with even bigger falls in fish

Bans on two of the most harmful PFAS are clearly working, Environment Agency tests show.

accha? daily · 17 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
−30%PFOS in English rivers
  • PFOA in rivers: −29%
  • PFAS types monitored: 60
  • PFOS banned: 2010

The findings

Recent testing by England's Environment Agency found levels of PFOS in rivers fell 30% and PFOA 29% over roughly the last ten years, Good News Network reports. Levels fell even faster in the flesh of freshwater fish.

PFOS was almost completely banned in the UK in 2010 and PFOA in 2020. Both are 'forever chemicals' that barely break down and have been linked to health risks.

Why it matters

It's proof that banning harmful chemicals does clean up nature. The agency now routinely tests for 60 kinds of PFAS in rivers, lakes, groundwater, coasts and seafood. One scientist told the BBC it was 'brilliant to see' the progress.
