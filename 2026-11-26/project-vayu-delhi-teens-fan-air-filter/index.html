Two Delhi teens turn ordinary classroom fans into air purifiers with a ₹140 filter, now reaching about 20,000 students

Krish Aggarwal and Samarth Meattle's balloon-like filter slips over a fan in under a minute and traps fine dust.

accha? daily · 26 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
₹140cost of one fan filter
  • Fits on table or wall fans in under a minute
  • Pilot: 295 classrooms, about 8,000 students
  • Filters turn grey as they trap dust

A cheap fix for dirty air

Most schools can't afford air purifiers, so two Delhi teenagers built something that uses what classrooms already have: fans. Their Project Vayu filter is made of charged fibres that pull in tiny PM2.5 particles. When the fan runs, the cover puffs up like a balloon and cleans the air passing through.

It needs no wiring or tools. A filter company gave them the material for free, which keeps the cost at about ₹140.

Real classrooms

In the team's own tests, PM2.5 in a small closed room fell by over 90% in an hour, and by about 60% in a bigger classroom. Results vary with room size.

A pilot covered 295 classrooms and about 8,000 children. The filters are now in more than 10 schools and some small clinics. Krish was named to the 2026 Diana Award Roll of Honour for the work.
