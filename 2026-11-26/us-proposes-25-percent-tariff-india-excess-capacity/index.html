US proposes tariffs of up to 25% on Indian goods over 'excess capacity', or 17% if India signs a trade deal

Washington named India among 14 economies it says flood the world with cheap factory goods. India says it has no such excess capacity and wants the issue settled in trade talks.

What the US wants

The US Trade Representative on Wednesday proposed new tariffs on 14 economies, including India, China, Japan and the European Union, after an eight-month probe under Section 301 of US trade law. It says these countries have 'structural excess capacity' in manufacturing that hurts American factories.

For India, the proposed rate is up to 25%, counted after existing Section 301 duties, with some products exempted. If India signs a trade deal that addresses the findings, the rate could drop to 17%. Vietnam faces the highest proposed rate among the group, up to 35%.

India pushes back

The Commerce Ministry said India 'does not have structural excess capacity in any sector' and that it engaged constructively during the probe. It wants the matter resolved through the India–US Bilateral Trade Agreement talks, which it hopes to finish soon.

What happens next

Nothing changes yet. Public comments are open until 5 January, hearings start on 25 January, and the duties could take effect as early as February.

The proposal gives both governments a fresh reason to speed up the trade talks: a signed deal would cut the proposed rate for India by 8 percentage points, from 25% to 17%. Exporters will be watching the January hearings closely, since the final rates and the list of exempted products are still to be decided.
