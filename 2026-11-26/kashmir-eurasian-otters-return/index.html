Once hunted almost to nothing for fur, Eurasian otters are showing up again in Kashmir's rivers, new studies show

Videos, camera traps and field surveys have found the shy river animal back in several streams of the Kashmir Valley.

accha? daily · 26 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
21otter signs on a 12-km river stretch
  • Species: Eurasian otter (Lutra lutra), listed as Near Threatened
  • Found in Lidder, Rambiara and Sindh streams
  • 92% of locals surveyed support protecting it

Back in the water

The Eurasian otter once lived all along the Jhelum basin, but the fur trade, pollution and lost habitat pushed it close to vanishing from Kashmir. Now it is turning up again.

A study published in the journal Biological Diversity in October calls the return a conservation win. A separate survey led by Wildlife Trust of India and the J&K wildlife department in Hirpora Wildlife Sanctuary found 21 signs of otters, including a direct sighting, droppings, pawprints and camera-trap photos, on a 12-km stretch of river.

What it needs now

Otters are top predators, so their presence is a good sign for clean rivers. But only 15% of locals surveyed could recognise one, even though 92% backed protecting it.

Researchers say the next steps are cleaner water, an end to fishing with chemicals and electric currents, and school and village awareness drives. Nobody yet knows how many otters live in the valley.
