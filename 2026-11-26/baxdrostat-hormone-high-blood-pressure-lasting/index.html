A 6–12 month drug course reversed a hormone-driven type of high blood pressure for up to 2 years in a small study

Nearly a quarter of the 14 patients had normal blood pressure with no pills at all after treatment ended.

accha? daily · 26 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
25 pointsaverage drop in blood pressure
  • Drug class: aldosterone synthase inhibitors, such as baxdrostat
  • 14 patients at 8 US centres
  • A bigger trial, ELIXIR, comes next

A possible cure for some

About 5–10% of people with high blood pressure have it because their body makes too much of a hormone called aldosterone. Until now, the only cure was surgery to remove an adrenal gland.

Researchers led by Queen Mary University of London gave 14 such patients in the US a longer course of a new type of drug, which blocks the hormone, for 6 to 12 months. Results were published in the New England Journal of Medicine.

What they found

Up to two years after stopping, the patients' hormone marker stayed about 99% lower and their blood pressure was about 25 points lower on average. Just under a quarter needed no medicine at all.

The study was small, and it will not help most people with high blood pressure. A bigger trial called ELIXIR will now test if the effect lasts.
