Flipkart CEO Kalyan Krishnamurthy steps down after 11 years as Walmart probe and IPO delays hang over the company

Flipkart has confirmed that its group CEO is leaving. Chief product and technology officer Balaji Thiagarajan will run the company until a new CEO is named in the coming weeks.

What happened

Flipkart said on Wednesday, 25 November, that group CEO Kalyan Krishnamurthy is stepping down after more than 11 years at the company. It is the biggest leadership change at India's largest homegrown e-commerce company since Walmart bought it in 2018.

Balaji Thiagarajan, Flipkart's chief product and technology officer, will lead the business for now. Flipkart said it will name a permanent successor "in the coming weeks". The company did not give a last working day, but Bloomberg reported that he will leave on 4 December.

Why now

Flipkart gave no reason. In a message, Krishnamurthy said strong ethics, integrity and high standards of governance had been central to Flipkart's growth.

Moneycontrol reported that Walmart's leadership asked him to step down, though other outlets could not confirm this. Reports say Walmart is probing claims that some Flipkart staff took kickbacks of over ₹150 crore from brands in exchange for better treatment on the platform. Walmart, which owns about 80% of Flipkart, has also reportedly asked it to delay its IPO until it turns EBITDA-profitable.

His time in charge

Krishnamurthy became CEO in 2017. Walmart bought control of Flipkart in a $16 billion deal the next year, and both co-founders, Sachin and Binny Bansal, later left. Flipkart says the business grew about fivefold while he was in charge, and it launched the quick-commerce service Flipkart Minutes on his watch.

Losses are still large. Flipkart Group reported a net loss of ₹8,468 crore for FY26, about 2% lower than the ₹8,646 crore it lost a year earlier.

What it means

The change comes as Flipkart moves its holding company to India to prepare for a stock market listing, and as it gets ready to enter food delivery and live ticketing. Whoever takes over will have to cut losses, deal with the probe and take on Amazon, Meesho and the quick-commerce apps.
