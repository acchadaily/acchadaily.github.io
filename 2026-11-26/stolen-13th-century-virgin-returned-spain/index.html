A 13th-century carved Virgin stolen from a Spanish village church in 1979 is found in the UK and handed back

The walnut statue, taken by the famous art thief known as 'Erik the Belgian', was returned by its owner as soon as police explained its history.

accha? daily · 26 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
47 yearsafter it was stolen
  • Made of walnut, with traces of its original gold paint
  • Stolen from Navarre, Spain, in 1979
  • Case reopened in April 2026

A long trail

The 54 cm statue of the Virgin Mary holding baby Jesus was carved from walnut in the 1200s. It vanished in 1979 from the small church of San Pedro de Meoz in Navarre, Spain, during a wave of thefts from village churches.

Spain's National Police reopened the case in April. They traced the statue through dealers in Belgium, London and the Netherlands to a private collection in Britain.

Coming home

The owner agreed to give it back right away. The handover took place at the Spanish Embassy in London, with help from London's Metropolitan Police.

The theft is blamed on René Alphonse van den Berghe, known as 'Erik the Belgian', who stole many pieces of church art in the 1970s and 80s. He died in 2020.
