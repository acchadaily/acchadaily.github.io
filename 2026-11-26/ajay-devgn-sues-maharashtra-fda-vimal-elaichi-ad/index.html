Ajay Devgn sues Maharashtra's food regulator over Vimal elaichi ads, says cardamom isn't banned; Hindi ad has 107 million views

Maharashtra told Devgn, Shah Rukh Khan and Tiger Shroff to stop promoting Vimal cardamom, saying the brand is really known for banned pan masala. Devgn has gone to court, and the case could decide how far surrogate ads can go.

The fight

Actor Ajay Devgn has sued Maharashtra's food safety authority after it told him to stop appearing in ads for Vimal Elaichi, a saffron-flavoured cardamom mouth freshener, Reuters reported on 26 November. The case is before the High Court in Mumbai.

In August, the state's food safety commissioner Tukaram Mundhe asked Devgn, Shah Rukh Khan and Tiger Shroff to stop endorsing the product. The state says Vimal is mainly known as a pan masala brand, and pan masala is banned in Maharashtra.

Both sides

In his court filing, dated 15 October, Devgn argues that cardamom itself is not banned, so there is "no bar" on endorsing it. Mundhe told Reuters the state believes the ads harm public health. The state is separately looking at replies from Khan and Shroff.

PB Agro, which holds the licence for Vimal cardamom and pan masala, said through its lawyer that the claims are "futile". It says it does not promote Vimal in Maharashtra and only runs national TV ads that cannot be limited to certain states.

Why brands are watching

This is a test case for surrogate advertising, where a restricted product like tobacco or liquor is promoted through a 'safe' product with the same brand name, such as soda, water or mouth freshener. India has been trying to finish rules that would ban such ads outright since 2024.

The money involved is big. India's pan masala market was worth about $5 billion last year, by industry estimates, and endorsements can make up as much as 30% of an actor's income. The Hindi version of the Holi-themed Vimal ad with all three stars has about 107 million views on YouTube. A court ruling could change how many brands run their ads.
