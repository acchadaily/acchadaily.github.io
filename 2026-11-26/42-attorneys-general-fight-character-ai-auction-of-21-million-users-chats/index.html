42 US attorneys general move to block bankrupt Character.AI from auctioning chats of 21 million users, including 2.1 million minors

The chatbot company's assets, including private chat histories, are set to be auctioned on 1 December. States say the buyer would not have to follow its privacy promises.

What is being sold

Character.AI, the app where people chat with AI characters, has filed for bankruptcy in a Delaware court. It plans to sell nearly all its assets in an auction on 1 December, including user account details and chat histories from more than 21 million US accounts. Over 2.1 million of these are registered to minors.

The only named bidder is Smirks and Pangs Acquisition LLC, a Delaware company formed on 13 November. The sale papers do not name who owns it.

What the states want

Attorneys general from 42 states and DC have asked the court to reject the sale terms and the lead bidder, and to deny a proposed $350,000 break-up fee. At the very least, they want children's chats and other sensitive data removed from the sale.

They point out that Character.AI's own privacy policy said it does not sell personal information for money, yet the buyer would not have to honour that or delete data on request. "No Marylanders' privacy, especially our children's, should be for sale," said Maryland's Anthony Brown.
