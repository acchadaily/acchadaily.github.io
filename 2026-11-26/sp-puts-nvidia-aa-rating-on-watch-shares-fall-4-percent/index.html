S&P warns it may cut Nvidia's AA credit rating over board oversight after Jensen Huang's return; shares slide about 4%

The rating agency says Huang's exit and quick reinstatement leave open questions about how the world's top AI chipmaker is governed. It will decide within 90 days.

The warning

S&P Global Ratings on Wednesday, 25 November, placed Nvidia's AA credit rating on 'CreditWatch negative', a formal sign that a downgrade could follow. AA is the third-highest grade on S&P's scale. Any cut would most likely be one notch, to AA-, unless new investigations or disclosures hurt the company.

S&P said Nvidia's business and finances are unchanged for now. Its concern is governance: Huang resigned on 12 November over conduct that broke the company's code, and the board brought him back on 23 November. S&P said this does not fully answer its questions about board oversight.

Weak spots and market reaction

S&P flagged three issues: long-serving directors whose independence may be weaker, no succession plan, and exposure to China and export controls. It also cut its view of Nvidia's management and governance from positive to neutral.

Nvidia shares fell about 4% on Wednesday, after jumping about 31% on Monday when Huang returned. The tech-heavy Nasdaq slipped too, as doubts about AI spending returned. Huang faces a closed-door interview with three House committees on 1 December.

💡 Explained

What is a credit rating 'CreditWatch'?

A credit rating tells lenders how safe it is to lend money to a company. When an agency like S&P puts a rating on 'CreditWatch negative', it is warning that it may soon lower that grade, usually within about 90 days, while it studies a new event. It is not a downgrade yet. For a company, a lower rating can mean paying a little more interest when it borrows. For Nvidia, S&P's worry is how the board oversees its CEO, not its cash.
