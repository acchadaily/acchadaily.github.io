A techie who left the US turns her father's leftover amla into 25+ products, now sold to over 4,000 customers

Kalaiselvi's Aruvi Eco Organics in Tamil Nadu uses about 60% of the farm's spare fruit and gives steady jobs to local workers.

accha? daily · 26 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
25+products made from farm surplus
  • Farm started by her father in 1999
  • Products are lab-tested at TNAU, Coimbatore
  • Sold on its website, Amazon and WhatsApp

From code to candy

Kalaiselvi spent nearly ten years working in IT in the US. During the pandemic, she spent time on her family's chemical-free farm in Iduvai, Tamil Nadu, and saw how much amla, sapota and mango went to waste each season.

She started drying fruit with a ₹28,000 dryer and trained in food processing for about two years, starting after she turned 40. Her business, Aruvi Eco Organics, now makes amla candy sweetened with jaggery, dried fruit, leaf powders and herbal teas in cotton bags.

Less waste, more jobs

She estimates the business now uses nearly 60% of the farm's usable surplus. By December 2024 it had more than 4,000 customers, selling online and at markets.

A six-person team runs it, including a single mother who earns ₹5,000 a week. Kalaiselvi wants to work with more local farmers next.
