Supreme Court clears bike taxis in Karnataka, saying it is 'better to regulate it than ban it' — a big win for Rapido, Uber and Ola

The top court dismissed Karnataka's appeal, ending a ban that shut bike taxis in Bengaluru and the rest of the state in June 2025.

In short

  • Ban in Karnataka since 16 June 2025
  • Bike taxis already run in 25 states
  • Rapido: 5 million+ rides a day, valued at $3 billion

What the court said

A bench of Justices Satish Chandra Sharma and N Kotiswar Singh dismissed the Karnataka government's appeal against a High Court judgment from 23 January. The High Court had said the state can regulate bike taxis but cannot refuse permits just because the vehicle has two wheels.

"It is better to regulate it than ban it," Justice Sharma said, noting that bike taxis already run in 25 states.

Why it matters

Bike taxis in Karnataka had been stopped since 16 June 2025. The aggregators told the court about 4.5 lakh passengers in the state use them, while driver groups say more than one lakh gig workers lost income during the shutdown.

For Bengaluru, a city famous for its traffic jams, a bike is often the fastest and cheapest way to get across town. Commuters had been forced back to autos, cabs and crowded buses.

Who wins

Rapido is the biggest winner. It operates in more than 400 cities, does over 5 million rides a day, and was valued at $3 billion after raising $240 million in May. Uber, whose Uber Bike runs in 100 cities, called the ruling "much-needed regulatory clarity". Ola also runs bike taxis.

The ruling also lands a day after Union minister Nitin Gadkari urged states to allow private bikes to be used as app taxis. Karnataka must now frame rules under which bike taxis can get permits, rather than blocking them outright.
