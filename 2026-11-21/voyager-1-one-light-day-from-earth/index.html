Voyager 1 becomes the first spacecraft ever to be one light-day from Earth, and the Empire State Building lights up gold for it

Launched in 1977, the little probe is still talking to us — its messages now take a full day to arrive.

A cosmic milestone

NASA's Voyager 1 has become the first human-made object to reach a distance of one light-day from Earth, about 26 billion km (16 billion miles). Radio signals, travelling at the speed of light, now take about 24 hours to get from the spacecraft to NASA's antennas.

New York's Empire State Building was lit up in gold to celebrate.

Still going

Voyager 1 was launched in 1977 to study Jupiter and Saturn and became the first spacecraft to enter interstellar space in 2012. Nearly 50 years later, engineers keep it alive by switching off parts one by one to save power, and it still sends back science from beyond the Sun's bubble.
