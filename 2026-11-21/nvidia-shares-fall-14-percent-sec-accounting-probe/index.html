Nvidia shares crash 14% and lose about $491 billion in a day after reports of an SEC accounting probe; the Nasdaq falls 5.3%

It was Nvidia's worst day since the DeepSeek scare, and it dragged the whole AI trade down with it.

What happened

Nvidia's stock fell 14.01% on Friday to close at $125.53, wiping out about $491 billion of market value in one session. The company is now worth about $3.03 trillion, down from about $3.52 trillion the day before.

The trigger was a Reuters report, citing two people briefed on the matter, that enforcement staff at the US Securities and Exchange Commission are examining "potential accounting issues" at the chipmaker. The SEC declined to comment. An inquiry is not a finding of wrongdoing.

What could be in question

Reports have not said exactly which practices are under review. Analysts have pointed to Nvidia's change in how it reports revenue by region, and to the financing it guarantees for some customers, which stood at $118 billion at the end of the last quarter.

The Wall Street Journal first reported an SEC probe on 17 November, without details.

A rough few weeks

The stock is now down about 48% from its record close of $239.24 on 6 October. Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO in mid-November, with finance chief Colette Kress serving as interim CEO.

The market damage

The fall spread across tech. The Nasdaq dropped 5.28% to 22,617.73 and the S&P 500 lost 3.70% to 6,910.39, putting it in a technical correction. Broadcom fell about 12%.

For India, the AI sell-off has had an unexpected upside: foreign investors have been moving money out of US AI stocks and into Indian shares this month.

💡 Explained

What does an SEC probe mean for a company like Nvidia?

The SEC is America's stock market regulator. Its enforcement staff can ask a company for documents to check whether its accounts or statements to investors were accurate. An inquiry is only an investigation, not a finding of wrongdoing, and many end with no action. But investors hate uncertainty, so even reports of a probe can knock billions off a company's value.
