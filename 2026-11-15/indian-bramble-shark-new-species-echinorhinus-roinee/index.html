A deep-sea shark mistaken for decades for an Atlantic species is a new one: meet the Indian Bramble Shark

Indian scientists studied sharks landed in Kollam, Kerala, and found a separate Indian Ocean species.

accha? daily · 15 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
3 mhow long the new shark can grow
  • Depth: 350–450 m
  • Found at: Kollam, Kerala
  • Funded by: Deep Ocean Mission

The discovery

Scientists from the Zoological Survey of India and ICAR-CMFRI have named the shark Echinorhinus roinee. For decades it had been mistaken for the Atlantic bramble shark.

They studied sharks caught by accident by deep-sea shrimp trawlers at Sakthikulangara harbour in Kollam. The shark lives at about 350–450 m depth, is dark brown to black and covered in thorn-like skin.

Why it matters

DNA tests show it is a separate Indian Ocean lineage that may also live off Oman, Sri Lanka and Thailand. Getting the species right makes fishery data and conservation decisions more accurate.
