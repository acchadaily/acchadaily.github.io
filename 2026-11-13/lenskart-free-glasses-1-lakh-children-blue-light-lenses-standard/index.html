Lenskart will give free glasses to 1 lakh children on Children's Day and makes blue-light lenses standard at no extra cost

On its first anniversary as a listed company, Lenskart's Peyush Bansal shared a 'confession': the problem the company was started to solve is still unsolved.

accha? daily · 13 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
1 lakhchildren aged 5–12 get free glasses
  • Free glasses: first 1,00,000 children
  • Fitting fee: ₹199
  • Eyewear sold a year: about 3.2 crore units

The two announcements

From 12 November, Lenskart is fitting blue-light filtering lenses as standard on its glasses in India, with no extra charge. Bansal admitted that the science on blue light is still debated, but said customers should not have to pay more for it.

On 14 November, Children's Day, the first 1,00,000 eligible children aged 5 to 12 can get prescription glasses, frames and lenses, free. Families pay ₹199 as a fitting fee.

The 'confession'

Bansal made the announcements in a LinkedIn post and an open letter marking one year since Lenskart's stock market listing. "We built a ten thousand crore company," he wrote, adding that the one thing they started it for "is still unsolved": millions of Indians who need glasses still don't have them.

Why it matters

Lenskart sells about 3.2 crore pairs of eyewear a year in India and says it wants to reach 10 crore. Free glasses for children is both a social move and a smart brand play: it builds trust with parents and brings a new generation into its stores. Making blue-light lenses standard also takes away a common upsell that rivals charge for.
