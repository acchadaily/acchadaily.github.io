Retail inflation hits a two-year high of 6.09% in October as food prices jump 8.94%, breaching RBI's 6% ceiling

Prices are rising faster than the RBI's comfort zone allows, and economists now expect another interest rate hike in December.

accha? daily · 13 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
6.09%retail inflation in October
  • Retail inflation: 6.09% (Sept: 5.54%)
  • Food inflation: 8.94%
  • Next RBI decision: 4 December

The numbers

India's consumer price inflation rose to 6.09% in October from 5.54% in September, government data released on 12 November showed. That is the highest in two years and above the RBI's upper tolerance limit of 6%. It is the first breach under the new CPI series, which uses 2024 as its base year.

Food inflation climbed to 8.94% from 7.74%. It was 9.09% in villages and 8.68% in cities. Overall inflation was higher in rural India (6.63%) than in urban India (5.41%). Eating out and hotel stays rose 10.06%.

What's pushing prices up

Vegetables, especially onions, have become much costlier, and higher fuel costs linked to the West Asia war are feeding into transport and food. Milk is getting dearer too: Amul and Mother Dairy raised prices by ₹2–4 a litre this week.

What it means for you

The RBI already raised its repo rate by 0.25 percentage points to 5.50% in October. DBS economist Radhika Rao expects a "second successive rate hike" at the 4 December policy meeting, and Axis Bank's Tanay Dalal says inflation could stay above 6% until June 2027. That means loan EMIs may rise further, while fixed deposit rates could improve. November's inflation number is due on 14 December.
