Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia CEO after 33 years; CFO Colette Kress named interim chief

The man who built the world's most valuable chip company has quit with immediate effect, days after Nvidia's board opened an independent probe.

What happened

Nvidia said on Thursday, 12 November, that Jensen Huang has stepped down as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately. According to the company's filing, the board accepted his resignation a day earlier. Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and ran it for 33 years, turning a graphics-card maker into the company at the centre of the AI boom.

Chief financial officer Colette Kress will be interim CEO while also staying CFO. The board says it has "full confidence" in her and will look at both internal and external candidates for the permanent job. Harvey C. Jones becomes Nvidia's first-ever board chair.

Why he left

Nvidia did not give a reason in its announcement. The exit comes days after the board launched an independent review on 8 November into reports that Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort. Media reports linked this to a woman US intelligence believes to be a Chinese spy, and Huang had publicly apologised for being unfaithful to his wife. Huang has agreed to testify before Congress.

The market reaction

Nvidia shares fell more than 13% at Thursday's open and briefly took the company's value below $4 trillion, then recovered much of the drop to close about 4% lower. The stock was already well below its 6 October peak. Nvidia reports its quarterly results on 17 November, which will be Kress's first big test.

The change also matters for other AI companies: Nvidia had agreed to buy about $10 billion of Anthropic shares alongside the Claude maker's IPO, and Bloomberg reports that pledge is now under scrutiny.
