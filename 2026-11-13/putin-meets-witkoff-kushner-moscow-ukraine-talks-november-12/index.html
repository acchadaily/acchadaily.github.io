Putin meets US envoys Witkoff and Kushner for over two hours; talks will continue, but no deal on territory

Direct US–Russia talks have resumed after a nine-month pause, and Europe is set to join the next round.

What happened

US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner met Vladimir Putin at the Kremlin on 12 November for more than two hours. The Kremlin called the talks "useful". The US backed Putin's idea of a joint humanitarian mission to a city in eastern Ukraine. "We've restarted it. We've set a new meeting," Witkoff said, adding that European countries would join the next round.

Where they still disagree

Putin rejected any foreign or European troops in Ukraine and Kyiv's idea of 'gray zones', insisting on a clear state border as the dividing line. Kushner said "significant differences remain". Kremlin aide Yuri Ushakov said Putin will pick up the talks with Donald Trump at the APEC summit in China later this month. The envoys then briefed Ukraine's President Zelensky in Dublin, who called the agreed prisoner swap the biggest of the war.
