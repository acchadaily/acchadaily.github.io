World first: bone marrow from a deceased organ donor cures a woman's aplastic anaemia

Six months on, the 40-year-old no longer needs blood or platelet transfusions.

accha? daily · 16 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
6 monthstransfusion-free after the transplant
  • Patient age: 40
  • Illness: 10 years
  • Complications: none reported

The breakthrough

Doctors at Franciscan Health in the US used bone marrow banked from a deceased organ donor by Ossium Health to treat a 40-year-old woman with a 10-year history of aplastic anaemia, a condition where the marrow stops making enough blood cells. It is the first reported use of deceased-donor marrow for a non-cancer disease.

Her disease had stopped responding to several treatments.

Why it matters

Six months after the April transplant her blood counts are restored, she needs no transfusions, and no complications have been reported. Banked marrow could widen the pool of donors for patients who can't find a living match.
