Denmark's parliament approves a security deal with Greenland and the US 109–0, hoping to end the row over Trump's Greenland threats

The pact lets the US expand its military presence on the island, but only NATO members can set up bases there.

accha? daily · 20 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
109–0vote in Denmark's parliament
  • Danish vote: 109–0
  • Greenland vote: passed by 16, with 7 abstentions
  • Builds on a 1951 defence pact

What's in the deal

The agreement, signed by the three governments in September, builds on a 1951 defence pact. It lets the US 'modernize and expand' its work at the Pituffik Space Base in northwest Greenland and says only NATO members can set up military installations on the island.

Greenland's own parliament backed it earlier this month by 16 votes, with seven abstentions.

Why it matters

The deal is meant to end tensions after Trump repeatedly talked about taking control of the mineral-rich island. It does not need a US Senate vote, and it would still apply if Greenland one day becomes independent. It takes effect once the US is formally notified.
