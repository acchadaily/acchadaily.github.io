US regulator lets SpaceX launch up to 1 million satellites for 'Starmind', its plan for AI data centres in space

Each solar-powered satellite would be about 70 m across. The first must be working by November 2033.

accha? daily · 27 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
1 millionsatellites allowed under the approval
  • Altitude: 500–1,002 km
  • Up to 250 kW of solar power per satellite
  • Deadline for first satellite: 25 Nov 2033

What was approved

The US Federal Communications Commission (FCC) gave SpaceX conditional approval to launch "up to one million satellites" for Starmind, a network of AI data centres in orbit, New Atlas reported on 25 November. The satellites would send results to Earth through the Starlink network using lasers.

They would fly 500–1,002 km up. Each would be 70–75 m across when unfolded, with a computer rack, a large radiator to shed heat and solar panels making up to 250 kW.

The catch

SpaceX must launch and run its first Starmind satellite by 25 November 2033 or lose the licence. Scientists question the cost and the cooling, and worry about space debris and the effect on astronomy. Big tech firms are hunting for power for AI, and space data centres are one bold idea.
