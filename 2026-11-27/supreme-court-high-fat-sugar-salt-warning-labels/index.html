Supreme Court orders separate 'HIGH FAT', 'HIGH SUGAR' and 'HIGH SALT' warnings on the front of packaged foods

The court went further than food regulator FSSAI's plan, asking for a separate warning for each nutrient. It is a big change for chips, biscuit and drink brands.

What the court said

A Supreme Court bench of Justices JB Pardiwala and K Vinod Chandran told the Centre and the Food Safety and Standards Authority of India (FSSAI) to require a separate picture warning on packs for each nutrient: "HIGH FAT", "HIGH SUGAR" and "HIGH SALT", Inc42 reported on 26 November, citing LiveLaw.

If a product is high in more than one, it must carry a separate hexagon for each. That is stricter than FSSAI's own plan, which would have put them all in one hexagon.

FSSAI's plan

FSSAI's revised plan uses limits from the ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines 2024 to decide what counts as 'high'. It also proposes a label saying a product contains non-caloric sweeteners.

FSSAI had asked for about four months to finish the draft rules, and suggested a 365-day period after that when following them would be voluntary.

What it means for brands

Front-of-pack warnings are among the toughest rules a food brand can face, because the warning sits right next to the logo. Countries like Chile and Mexico use black warning labels, and studies there linked them to shoppers buying fewer sugary products.

Indian brands are already under pressure. Inc42 says at least 20 D2C food brands have received FSSAI notices since May over claims like "no added sugar" and "natural". Ramen brand MasterChow dropped "100% Natural" from its packs.

What's next

The court also wants nutrition to be taught in schools. The final rules and dates will come from FSSAI, so expect packaging redesigns and recipe changes as brands try to stay below the limits.

💡 Explained

What is a front-of-pack warning label?

It is a simple symbol printed on the front of a food packet, next to the brand name, that tells you at a glance if the food has a lot of fat, sugar or salt. Nutrition tables on the back are small and hard to read, so many countries now use bold front labels instead. India's Supreme Court wants a separate hexagon for each problem nutrient, so a snack high in both salt and fat would carry two warnings.
