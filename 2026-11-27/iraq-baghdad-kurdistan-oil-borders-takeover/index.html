Iraq moves to take control of Kurdish oil fields and borders, saying 200,000+ barrels a day are smuggled; big protests in Erbil

Iraq's top court backed Baghdad. Kurdish leaders call it a 'red line', and national talks are set for 1 December.

What Baghdad did

On 24 November, Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered federal control of all border crossings into the Kurdistan Region and told the Oil Ministry to take over oil fields in Kirkuk, Nineveh, Salahuddin and Diyala. A day later, Iraq's Federal Supreme Court backed Baghdad's authority over borders and oil and gas revenues.

The government says 200,000–230,000 barrels of oil a day are smuggled, worth about $200 million a month.

The pushback

The Kurdistan Regional Government called the moves unconstitutional, and Kurdish parties called them a "red line". Tens of thousands protested in Erbil. Baghdad denied reports that troops were heading to the region, and Iraq's top leaders agreed to a national dialogue on 1 December.
