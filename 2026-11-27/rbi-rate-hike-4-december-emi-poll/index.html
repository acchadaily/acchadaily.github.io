Loan EMIs may rise again: over 90% of economists expect the RBI to raise the repo rate to 5.75% on 4 December

Retail inflation hit a two-year high of 6.09% in October, above the RBI's comfort zone. Markets see a small chance of an even bigger hike.

accha? daily · 27 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
5.75%expected repo rate after a 25 bps hike
  • MPC decision: 4 December
  • October CPI inflation: 6.09% (September: 5.54%)
  • Current repo rate: 5.50%

What's expected

The RBI's Monetary Policy Committee meets from 2 to 4 December. More than 90% of economists in a Reuters poll expect a 25 basis-point hike, taking the repo rate from 5.50% to 5.75%. Market prices show an outside chance of a 50 basis-point move.

The RBI raised rates by 25 basis points in October, its first hike in about four years. October's retail inflation of 6.09% was above the RBI's upper limit of 6%.

What it means for you

When the repo rate goes up, banks usually raise interest on floating-rate home, car and personal loans, so EMIs rise or loan terms get longer. Fixed-deposit rates often go up too, which is good news for savers.

SBI Research supports a 25 basis-point hike, but warns that a bigger one could hurt growth and the rupee.

💡 Explained

What is the repo rate, and what is a basis point?

The repo rate is the interest rate at which the Reserve Bank of India lends money to banks for short periods. When it goes up, borrowing becomes costlier for banks, and they usually pass that on by raising loan rates, which pushes up EMIs. A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point, so a 25 basis-point hike means a rise of 0.25%, for example from 5.50% to 5.75%.
