China's trade surplus passes $1 trillion for the second year running, with two months still to go

October exports jumped 35.2% from a year earlier, driven by demand for AI and data-centre equipment.

accha? daily · 11 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
$1.04TChina's surplus, Jan–Oct 2026
  • Oct exports: +35.2%
  • Oct imports: +41.6%
  • Oct surplus: $105.3bn

Record pace

Customs data released on 10 November show China sold $1.04 trillion more goods than it bought in the first ten months of 2026. It has never reached that mark this early. The full-year surplus for 2025 was about $1.2 trillion, a record, and economists at ING expect 2026 to top it.

Driven by AI

October exports rose 35.2% to about $412 billion, beating forecasts. Shipments of cables, server racks and other data-centre gear rose sharply. High-tech exports grew 67%, the fastest since early 2021. Imports also climbed 41.6%, so the monthly surplus of $105.3 billion was smaller than in September.

Why it matters

The huge surplus is adding to pressure on Beijing from the US, Europe and others to buy more from abroad. China's exports to both the US and the EU rose strongly in October.
