Rediff, India's 1996 internet pioneer, files updated papers for a ₹600 crore IPO after its profit jumps nearly 9x

The Rediffmail maker, now owned by AvenuesAI (formerly Infibeam), wants to spend the money on payments, video and enterprise apps, plus ₹183 crore on marketing.

  • ₹183.34 crore earmarked for marketing

What happened

Rediff has filed updated draft papers with SEBI for a ₹600 crore IPO, about two months after the regulator cleared its confidential filing. All of it will be new shares, so no existing owner is selling.

About ₹224.8 crore will go into its business app Rediff One, Rediff Pay and Rediff TV, and ₹183.34 crore into marketing and branding.

The comeback numbers

Revenue grew 258.7% to ₹133.6 crore in FY26, and net profit rose almost ninefold to ₹55.9 crore. In the June 2026 quarter alone, it made ₹35.2 crore profit on ₹58.4 crore revenue.

Founded by Ajit Balakrishnan in 1996, Rediff launched Rediffmail in 1998. Infibeam bought a majority stake, and Balakrishnan retired in 2024.

Its operating profit margin (EBITDA) swung from a loss in FY25 to nearly 49% in FY26, though its net debt rose to about ₹355 crore by June 2026.
