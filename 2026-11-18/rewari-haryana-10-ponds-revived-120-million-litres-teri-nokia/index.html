Water-stressed Rewari will revive 10 village ponds to hold 120 million litres of rainwater, helping about 22,000 people

TERI and Nokia India have started a two-year project to clean up and green old ponds in a Haryana district where groundwater keeps falling.

What happened

Haryana's Rewari district has been listed as water-stressed since 2019, and groundwater in its Khol block is being pumped faster than it refills. Now the research body TERI and Nokia India have signed a deal to bring about 10 village ponds back to life over the next 24 months.

Together the ponds are meant to hold 1.2 lakh cubic metres of water, or 120 million litres. That is a target for the project, not water already saved.

How it will work

Many old ponds have filled up with silt and rubbish, and their inlets are blocked, so rain runs off instead of soaking into the ground. The team will plant native trees, grasses and shrubs on the banks to hold the soil and filter dirty water, and add 100 streetlights, benches and dustbins around the ponds.

Village panchayats and residents will help track water levels and report problems. About 22,000 people are expected to benefit directly, and around 50,000 indirectly.
