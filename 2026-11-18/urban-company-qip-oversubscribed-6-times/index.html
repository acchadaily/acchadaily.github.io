Urban Company's ₹2,500 crore share sale is oversubscribed about 6 times; SBI MF, ICICI Prudential, Goldman Sachs and Norges bid

Big investors rushed in on day one. Urban Company will use ₹600 crore of the money to fight Snabbit and Pronto in the 'maid in minutes' race.

accha? daily · 18 November 2026 · 2 min read
WhatsApp
In short
6xtimes the QIP was oversubscribed
  • QIP size ₹2,500 crore at a floor price of ₹161.03
  • ₹600 crore set aside for InstaHelp
  • New-age firms have raised ₹25,200 crore+ via QIPs since 2021

What happened

Urban Company's ₹2,500 crore QIP, a share sale only for big institutions, drew bids worth about six times the amount on offer, Moneycontrol reported on 17 November. The floor price was ₹161.03 a share.

Bidders included SBI, ICICI Prudential, UTI, Aditya Birla Sun Life, Mirae and Invesco mutual funds, insurers HDFC Life and Bajaj Life, and long-term global funds like Goldman Sachs Asset Management, Norway's Norges and Amundi.

Where the money goes

About ₹600 crore, roughly a quarter, is set aside for InstaHelp, the app's quick home-help service for cleaning and dishwashing. Rivals Snabbit (backed by Lightspeed) and Pronto (backed by General Catalyst) are spending heavily on discounts too.

Urban Company has said it will put market share ahead of short-term profit in InstaHelp. Moneycontrol notes it has burned more cash in one quarter than Snabbit did in a full year. Snabbit lost ₹167.75 crore in FY26 on just ₹12.25 crore of revenue.

The bigger trend

Listed new-age companies keep coming back to big investors for money. Together they have raised or announced more than ₹25,200 crore through QIPs since 2021. Zomato raised ₹8,500 crore this way in 2024 and Swiggy ₹10,000 crore in 2025.

A QIP lets a listed company raise money quickly without a fresh public offer. For Urban Company, strong demand from big funds is a vote of confidence in its bet that Indians will book house help as easily as they order groceries.
