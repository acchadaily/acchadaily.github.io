A Srinagar teacher quit after 19 years to sell home-cooked meals with one tiffin box; her Ghar Batti now caters events for 200 guests

Ruksana Begum started at 48 with almost no money. Today her home kitchen handles 10–12 events a month and employs three women.

One tiffin box

Ruksana Begum taught for 19 years before resigning in 2019. That November, at 48, she started Ghar Batti Food Service from her home in Srinagar. 'Ghar' means home and 'batti' is the Kashmiri word for rice. She bought one tiffin box, used her own kitchen, and took orders that ranged from under ₹100 to ₹2,000.

About a year later, customers began asking her to cook for their events, and the business changed shape.

Now

Today, at 55, she caters 10 to 12 events a month for hospitals, schools, banks and offices, and can feed up to 200 guests at a time. The menu runs from rogan josh, yakhni and dum olav to chilli chicken, with harissa and smoked fish added in winter.

Her team has four people: three women who cook the big orders and one delivery worker. She buys from local butchers, fish sellers and vegetable vendors, and hopes to reach 20 events a month, still from her own kitchen.
