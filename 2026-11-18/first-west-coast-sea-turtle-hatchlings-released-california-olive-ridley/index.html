75 baby olive ridley turtles from the first sea turtle nests ever recorded on the US West Coast crawl into the Pacific

The eggs were rescued from California beaches before a hurricane, hatched at an aquarium, and were released on 14 and 15 November.

A first for California

In mid-September, olive ridley sea turtles laid eggs on Seal Beach and Huntington Beach in Southern California. These were the first sea turtle nests ever recorded on America's West Coast. Olive ridleys usually nest on warmer shores, including Odisha's famous beaches in India.

With a hurricane on the way, biologists dug up the eggs on 25 September and moved them to the Aquarium of the Pacific in Long Beach, where they hatched.

Back to the sea

Before dawn on 14 November, the Seal Beach babies were measured and allowed to crawl into the water on their own. The Huntington Beach group followed the next morning, making 75 in all. Photos were taken under red light so the tiny turtles were not disturbed.

Scientists are not sure why the turtles picked California; a strong El Niño year is one idea. A US Fish and Wildlife official said decades of protection have helped olive ridley numbers grow in parts of their range. A third nest was found in Ventura County on 1 November.
