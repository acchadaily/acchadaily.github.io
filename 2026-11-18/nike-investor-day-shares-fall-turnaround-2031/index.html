Nike shares fall 6.3% after its first investor day since 2017; sales may keep shrinking for two more years

CEO Elliott Hill laid out a plan to grow again by fiscal 2029, but investors did not like the wait or the outlook for China.

accha? daily · 18 November 2026 · 2 min read

  • ~$12 billion in dividends planned over four years

What happened

Nike held its first investor day in nine years on 17 November and shared a turnaround plan called 'Full Stride Ahead'. The stock jumped early in the day, then fell to close at $35.85, down 6.3%.

The problem was the timeline. Nike said sales could keep falling for about two more years, with a return to normal growth only in fiscal 2029. It called fiscal 2028 a 'challenging year' with slightly lower profit per share.

The plan

By fiscal 2031, Nike is aiming for about $5 billion in extra revenue, earnings of $3.00–$3.50 a share and an operating margin of 12–14%. A cost-cutting programme called Pace should save about $2.5 billion a year by then.

To keep shareholders patient, Nike plans to pay out about $12 billion in dividends over the next four years.

Why it matters

Greater China, once Nike's growth engine, is expected to stay flat from fiscal 2027 to 2031. Stifel noted that Nike's targets suggest about $48 billion in revenue, below its own estimate of $51.2 billion.

For shoe and sportswear fans in India and everywhere else, the message is that the world's biggest sportswear brand is still mid-reset. A separate Motley Fool analysis notes the stock is now about 78% below its all-time high, and Nike's own plan says real growth only returns in fiscal 2029.
