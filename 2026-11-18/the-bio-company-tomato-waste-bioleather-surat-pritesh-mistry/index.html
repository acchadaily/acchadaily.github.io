A 26-year-old's startup turns rotten tomatoes into vegan leather, now making 5,000 metres a month in Surat

Pritesh Mistry's The Bio Company uses tomato waste from three states to make a leather alternative for bags, jackets and shoes.

accha? daily · 18 November 2026 · 1 min read

In short
5,000 mof Bioleather made every month
  • India wastes about 30–35% of its tomatoes
  • First product launched in 2019
  • Made with no PU or PVC plastic

From waste to wallet

India grows about 44 million tonnes of tomatoes a year, the second-most in the world, but roughly 30–35% of them go to waste. Pritesh Mistry, a biotech graduate from Mumbai, started turning that waste into leather as a final-year college project after seeing both rotting farm produce and the pollution from Kanpur's tanneries.

His startup, The Bio Company, launched its first 'Original Bioleather' in 2019 and has patented its process.

How it is made

At its Surat plant, tomato leftovers from Maharashtra, Gujarat and Madhya Pradesh are mixed with plant-based binders and natural fibres, then given a plant-based water-resistant coat. There is no plastic like PU or PVC in it, and it uses natural dyes.

Fashion brands use it for jackets, bags and footwear, a Toronto handbag label moved to full production with it in 2024, and some car brands are testing it for seats. Mistry's idea also won PETA's Best Innovation in Textile award in 2021.
