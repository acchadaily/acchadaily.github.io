Two women turn the paddy straw farmers burn into fuel pellets, building a ₹7–8 crore-a-year business in Sirsa

Sanjana and Sonika's company buys stubble that would otherwise be set on fire and sells pellets to power plants.

From smoke to fuel

Sanjana from Ghoranwali village in Haryana's Sirsa district and Sonika from Bhadra in Rajasthan started SSAR Biomass Green in 2024. They invested about ₹2.5 crore with help from the government's Agriculture Infrastructure Fund to set up a unit in Ghoranwali.

The plant turns leftover paddy straw, or parali, into compact biofuel pellets sold to power plants. It handles about 1 to 1.5 lakh quintals of straw a year.

Why it matters

Burning stubble is a big cause of north India's winter smog. By paying for straw, the business gives farmers a reason not to burn it, creates work for local baler operators, and employs more than 20 people. Turnover is now ₹7–8 crore a year.
