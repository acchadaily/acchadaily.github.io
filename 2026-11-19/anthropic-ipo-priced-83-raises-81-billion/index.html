Anthropic prices its IPO at $83 a share, above its range, raising $81.3 billion; trading on Nasdaq as 'ANT' starts today

The Claude maker sold about 980 million shares, more than planned, valuing it at roughly $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis.

The deal

Anthropic priced its initial public offering at $83 a share on 18 November, above the $70–78 range it had marketed. It sold about 980 million Class A shares, up from a planned 825 million, raising $81.3 billion. Shares are due to start trading on Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker 'ANT', and the offering is expected to close on 20 November.

That is more than the $75 billion SpaceX raised in its base IPO deal in June (SpaceX reached $85.7 billion after extra shares were sold to underwriters). Google, Microsoft, Nvidia and AMD together agreed to buy $26 billion of stock at the IPO price.

The numbers in the filing

Revenue for the nine months to 30 September was $33.24 billion, up about 1,200% from a year earlier. Its run-rate revenue was about $81 billion at the end of October, up $10 billion in a month, CNBC reported.

The company also reported a $104 billion net loss for those nine months, mostly from changes in the value of convertible notes and derivatives rather than day-to-day operations.

Why it matters

At about $1.5 trillion, Anthropic joins the world's most valuable listed companies on day one. Its seven co-founders are selling about $1.96 billion of shares between them.

How the stock trades today will be read as a test of investor appetite for AI, after a year of huge spending on chips and data centres. (Disclosure: Anthropic makes Claude, the AI model that helps produce this site.)
