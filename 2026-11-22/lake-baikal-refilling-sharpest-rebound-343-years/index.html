Lake Baikal, which holds a fifth of the world's surface fresh water, is refilling at its fastest rate in 343 years

Tree rings show river inflows into the Siberian lake have bounced back sharply since 2017.

accha? daily · 22 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
145%of long-term average inflow since 2017
  • Baikal holds ~20% of surface fresh water
  • 343-year tree-ring record
  • Inflows once fell up to 90%

The study

Researchers led by Shijie Wang of Yunnan University used tree rings covering 343 years to rebuild the record of rivers feeding Lake Baikal. In the worst dry periods, inflows fell by up to 90%. Since 2017 they have run at 145% of the long-term average — the sharpest rebound in the whole record — and lake levels and plant growth are rising.

The paper was published in Geophysical Research Letters.

A note of caution

The authors link it to extra moisture from northern seas and slower Arctic warming in the region. They stress it is natural variation, not a sign that global warming is easing.
