Old Monk maker Mohan Meakin's revenue falls 64% to ₹288 crore after FSSAI says three variants can't be sold as 'rum'

India's most nostalgic rum brand is in a labelling fight with the food regulator, and it is showing up hard in the numbers.

The hit

Mohan Meakin, the company behind Old Monk, saw revenue for April–September fall to ₹288 crore from ₹792 crore a year earlier — a drop of about ₹504 crore, or 64%. Quarterly revenue fell even more sharply, from ₹473 crore to ₹108 crore, Mint reports, citing the Times of India.

The reason is not that people stopped liking Old Monk. It is a fight over what can legally be called rum.

What FSSAI objected to

The Food Safety and Standards Authority of India objected to some products made by blending neutral spirit with rum flavouring. In its view, those should be labelled a "rum-flavoured spirit", not rum.

It also questioned age claims on labels such as "7 Years Old Blended" and "Very Old Vatted". The company has agreed to drop the seven-year ageing claim.

Plant shut, workers waiting

Its bottling plant at Khopoli in Maharashtra has reportedly been shut since May. The bottler withdrew its petition in the Bombay High Court on 5 November and will submit new labels instead.

The company's lawyers put its losses at about ₹50 crore. Maharashtra is estimated to have lost about ₹350 crore in excise duty, and about 300 workers are without jobs while the plant stays closed.

A cult brand under pressure

Old Monk has long been a word-of-mouth legend that famously did little advertising. Its sales had already fallen once before, from 8 million cases in 2002 to 4.1 million in 2013, before a revival. Getting the new labels approved quickly is now the company's biggest task.
