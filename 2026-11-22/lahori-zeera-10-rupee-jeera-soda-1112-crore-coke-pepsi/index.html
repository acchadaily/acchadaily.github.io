₹10 jeera soda Lahori Zeera, started by three cousins in Punjab, expects about ₹1,112 crore in revenue as desi drinks eat into Coke and Pepsi

A cheap, spicy, very Indian fizzy drink has become one of the biggest challenger brands in the soft-drinks aisle.

From family money to a mass brand

Lahori Zeera was started in 2017 by cousins Saurabh Munjal, Nikhil Doda and Saurabh Bhutna in Punjab, using money from their families. Its main product is a cumin-flavoured soda sold for as little as ₹10.

The company now expects annual revenue of $115–120 million, or about ₹1,112 crore, according to a BBC report carried by Mint.

Taking share from the giants

Domestic brands held about 15% of India's carbonated drinks market by September 2025, a Kotak Mutual Fund report says. Over the same stretch, the combined share of Coca-Cola and PepsiCo fell from about 93% to 85%.

The jeera space is now crowded. Rivals include Reliance's Campa Jeera, Jeeru, Fizz Jeera Masala, Parle Agro's Dhishoom and Coca-Cola's own Rimzim, whose production Coca-Cola has reportedly doubled.

Why it works

Price is a big part of it: a ₹10 bottle fits small-town shops and impulse buys. Flavour is the other — a masala-style drink feels familiar to Indian taste buds in a way cola does not.

There is plenty of room to grow. India's non-alcoholic drinks market was worth about $32 billion in 2024 and could double by 2033.
