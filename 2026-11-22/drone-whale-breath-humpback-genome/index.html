Scientists read a humpback whale's whole genome from its breath, caught by a drone

No darts, no biopsies — just a drone carrying petri dishes through a whale's spout.

accha? daily · 22 November 2026 · 1 min read
WhatsApp
In short
84%of the DNA in the spray was whale DNA
  • 58 samples from 26 whales
  • 3 new DNA variants found
  • Published in Molecular Ecology Resources

How it worked

A team including the University of St Andrews flew a drone with sterile dishes through humpback whale blows in British Columbia's Kitimat Fjord System. They collected 58 samples from 26 whales — more than a third of them from land, without a boat.

About 84% of the DNA was whale DNA, enough to sequence whole genomes. The method sexed every whale correctly and found three new mitochondrial DNA variants.

Why it matters

Until now, only one North Pacific humpback whole genome was public. A gentle, cheap method like this could help track the health of whale populations without hurting them.
