Indian startup GG Technologies sues Apple for ₹1,500 crore, saying the iPhone's NameDrop copies its patented contact-sharing tech

The maker of the StayTouch app has gone to the Bombay High Court and wants NameDrop switched off in India.

The case

GG Technologies, the company behind the contact-sharing app StayTouch, has filed a patent suit against Apple in the Bombay High Court. It is asking for ₹1,500 crore in damages, a figure it calls a "conservative" estimate.

The startup was founded in 2018 by IIT Delhi alumnus Gaurav Goel. Its claim is simple: NameDrop, the iPhone feature that lets two people swap contact details by holding their phones close together, works the way its own invention does.

The patent at the centre

GG holds Indian Patent No. 546169, titled "Proximity-Based Contact Transfer", which was granted on 27 July 2024. The company says it started filing for protection in May 2019 — four years before Apple announced NameDrop in June 2023.

Beyond money, the suit asks the court to make Apple disable NameDrop in India, including through a software update, "to the extent technically feasible". GG says it spent about $2.75 million building and marketing its product between 2019 and 2025.

Not its first fight with Apple

This is not a one-off. GG filed a suit in the US in April 2026, which has been on hold since September while the US International Trade Commission looks into the matter. It filed another US case on 3 November.

No hearing date has been set in Bombay yet, and Apple had not responded publicly when the case was reported.

Why it matters

Small Indian tech firms rarely take on the world's most valuable brands in court over patents. If the case goes anywhere, it could be a test of how strongly Indian patents protect home-grown software ideas against global giants. For now, it is only a claim — the court has not ruled on anything.
