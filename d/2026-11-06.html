Blinkit Ambulance will now charge ₹2,500 for non-emergency trips

Emergency rides stay free. Routine trips like check-ups and hospital-to-home transfers will now be paid.

accha? daily · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read
₹0
charged for emergency rides — that doesn't change

What happened

Blinkit, the quick-commerce app owned by Eternal (formerly Zomato), runs its own ambulance service in a few cities. Until now, every ride was free.

On Thursday, Deepinder Goyal, Eternal's vice-chairman, announced that non-emergency trips will now cost ₹2,500 each. That covers things like routine check-ups, regular hospital visits and hospital-to-home transfers. The amount is collected after the trip and is roughly what private ambulances charge.

Emergency cases remain completely free.

Why they did it

According to Goyal, the service has done about 42,000 trips in two years, around 20,000 of them emergencies — and not one has been charged.

The problem: because rides were free, more and more people booked them for routine trips. Every routine booking takes an ambulance away from someone who might be having a heart attack or a stroke. The fee is meant to discourage that, so ambulances stay available for real emergencies.

The money collected goes to the Eternal Foundation, which runs the ambulances, to help expand the service.

Why it matters

It's a rare case of a startup adding a price to make a free service work better, not to make money. Many people on social media backed the move, saying a free service only works if people don't misuse it.

Key numbers
  • ₹2,500 per non-emergency trip
  • ~42,000 trips in two years
  • ~20,000 emergency trips, all free

Deepinder Goyal hits back at doctors over his ‘Temple’ wearable

Doctors called the ₹85,000 brain-tracking gadget a scam. Goyal says the science will prove them wrong.

accha? daily · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read
$20M
of his own money put into Temple, Goyal says

What is Temple?

Temple is a small wearable that sits near the temple on the side of your head. It comes from Deepinder Goyal, the founder of Zomato, who stepped back from running Eternal to focus on it. It is priced at ₹85,000.

Goyal describes it as a fitness and wellness tracker — not a medical device.

What the doctors are saying

Several doctors on X have questioned the product. Their main argument: the device sits over the superficial temporal artery, which carries blood to the face and scalp — so it can't really be measuring blood flow to the brain, as its marketing suggests.

Some, including the well-known liver specialist who posts as ‘TheLiverDoc’, went further and called it a scam, saying there is no solid evidence behind its claims for such a high price.

How Goyal responded

Goyal rejected the scam claims on Thursday. He said that if the product were a scam, people would find out very quickly, and that its most important features haven't even been made public yet.

He says he has put about $20 million of his own money into the venture, and points to a 14-day, no-questions-asked return policy for anyone who isn't happy.

Why it matters

It's a big test for one of India's best-known founders as he moves from food delivery into ‘deep tech’ health gadgets — a space where doctors and regulators expect hard scientific proof, not just good marketing.

Key numbers
  • Price: ₹85,000
  • Goyal's own investment: ~$20 million
  • 14-day no-questions return

OpenAI could go public in 2027

The ChatGPT maker's president says the company could be ready for a stock market listing next year.

accha? daily · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read
+70%
growth in annualised revenue last quarter

What happened

Speaking at a Wall Street Journal tech conference, OpenAI co-founder and president Greg Brockman was asked if the company could be ready for an IPO in 2027. His answer: “I think so. Yes.”

He added that OpenAI will list when the timing feels right, and that he feels very good about the business.

The honest part

Brockman admitted the first half of 2026 was “pretty rough” for OpenAI's business with companies. He also said OpenAI “wasn't even in the conversation” in AI coding agents — a space where rival Anthropic's Claude Code has been winning customers.

Things turned around in the third quarter: OpenAI's annualised revenue grew about 70% compared with the previous quarter, mainly because revenue from businesses doubled. Enterprise customers now bring in most of OpenAI's money.

Why it matters

An OpenAI IPO would be one of the biggest stock market listings ever, and it would let ordinary investors own a piece of the company behind ChatGPT for the first time. It also signals that the AI race is shifting from consumer chatbots to selling AI to businesses.

Key numbers
  • Possible IPO: 2027
  • Q3 annualised revenue: +70% vs Q2
  • Enterprise revenue: doubled

Viral

A viral video got a PVR juice counter shut in Bengaluru

A moviegoer alleged that used cups were being reused. Food safety officials inspected — and shut the counter.

accha? daily · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read

What happened

A customer at the PVR INOX multiplex in Forum Mall, South Bengaluru, posted a video on Instagram showing a stack of used disposable cups. He alleged that staff were reusing cups that customers had already drunk from.

The video spread quickly. On its own, a video can't prove the allegation — but it was enough to get officials to act.

What the authorities did

Karnataka food safety officers inspected the outlet. The state said they found violations of food safety rules, issued a notice, and shut the juice counter. The exact violations haven't been made public yet.

What the companies said

Forum South Bengaluru apologised to the customer and said the PVR team was investigating the complaint and would take action.

Why it matters

It shows how fast one customer's video can turn into government action against a big brand. For brands, the lesson is simple: in 2026, every customer is a reporter.

Key numbers
  • Where: Forum Mall, South Bengaluru
  • Action: notice issued, counter shut
  • Status: PVR investigating

India set for a ₹1 lakh crore Dhanteras

Traders expect record shopping today — led by gold and silver, even though gold is about 20% costlier than last year.

accha? daily · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read
₹1.42L
price of 24-carat gold per 10 grams

What's expected

The Confederation of All India Traders (CAIT) estimates that Indians will spend more than ₹1 lakh crore on Dhanteras today, the day traditionally seen as lucky for buying gold, silver, utensils and new things for the home.

Gold and silver alone are expected to bring in over ₹60,000 crore.

The price problem

24-carat gold is around ₹1,41,840 per 10 grams — roughly 20% more than a year ago. Jewellers say many buyers are switching to lighter 14- and 18-carat pieces so they can still buy something without spending too much.

The bigger picture

CAIT expects total festive-season trade, from Navratri to Diwali, to cross ₹7 lakh crore — 15–18% more than last year. That's a strong sign that people are still spending, even with higher prices.

What it means for you

If you're buying gold today, compare making charges across jewellers and always check the BIS hallmark. Lighter 18-carat designs are a popular way to keep the tradition without stretching the budget.

Key numbers
  • Dhanteras trade: ₹1 lakh crore+
  • Gold & silver: ₹60,000 crore+
  • Festive season: ₹7 lakh crore+

Cristiano Ronaldo retires from international football

After 23 years and a record 146 goals, the 41-year-old is done playing for Portugal — but he isn't leaving Al-Nassr.

accha? daily · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read
146
goals for Portugal, a national record

What happened

Cristiano Ronaldo announced on Thursday, in a video message, that he is retiring from international football. He called it “not an easy decision”.

He leaves after 234 matches for Portugal and 146 goals — the most in the team's history. He is also the only player to score at six different World Cups.

Why now

Portugal went out in the Round of 16 at the 2026 World Cup this summer, where Ronaldo scored three goals in five games. Reports say tensions with head coach Jorge Jesus had been building, and Ronaldo left the squad suddenly about five weeks ago.

What's next

He is not retiring from football completely. Ronaldo will keep playing for Al-Nassr in Saudi Arabia, where his contract runs until June 2027. He is on 985 career goals — and chasing 1,000.

Wall Street loses $2.5 trillion in a single day

The Nasdaq fell 5.5%, its worst day since April 2025, as AI and chip stocks crashed.

accha? daily · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read
−12.3%
chip stocks, worst day in ~6.5 years

What happened

US stocks had a brutal Thursday. The Nasdaq fell 5.52%, the S&P 500 fell 4.36% and the Dow dropped 1,833 points. About $2.5 trillion was wiped off the Nasdaq alone.

Chipmakers were hit hardest: Nvidia fell about 9%, while AMD and Micron fell around 17%.

Why it happened

After Democrats won both houses of Congress in the midterms, investors began to worry that new rules and local opposition could slow the building of AI data centers — the engine behind the AI boom. Weak results from Qualcomm added to the selling.

Why it matters

Indian markets opened lower on Friday too. Some analysts called it a knee-jerk reaction; others said the AI rally had become too speculative and needed to cool down.

Tankers catch fire in the Strait of Hormuz

Iran says at least five tankers and a tugboat caught fire, several after hitting mines. Oil crossed $100 a barrel.

accha? daily · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read
$100+
Brent crude, per barrel

What happened

Iran's forces said several oil tankers caught fire in the Strait of Hormuz on Thursday, some after hitting naval mines, and a tugboat sent to help also caught fire. A UK maritime agency separately reported a tanker hit by projectiles north of Qatar.

Why it matters

About a fifth of the world's oil normally passes through this narrow strait. Traffic has been heavily restricted since the war involving Iran began in February, and attacks on ships are near record levels. Qatar is mediating talks, but no ceasefire is in place.

What it means for you

India imports most of its oil. If crude stays above $100, it can push up fuel prices and inflation.

India

TRAI proposes separate licences for satellite internet

The telecom regulator wants satellite players under a new set of rules — and not selling directly to customers.

accha? daily · 6 November 2026 · 2 min read

What happened

The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India (TRAI) on Thursday recommended a separate authorisation for satellite communication providers, along with a ‘network-as-a-service’ model.

Under the proposal, satellite companies would not be allowed to sell telecom services directly to consumers.

Why it matters

Satellite internet is the next big battleground in Indian telecom. These rules will shape how players like Starlink, Jio and Airtel's satellite ventures reach customers. The government still has to decide whether to accept TRAI's recommendations.

Key numbers
  • Separate satellite authorisation
  • Network-as-a-service model
  • No direct-to-consumer sales
